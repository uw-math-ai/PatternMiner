# Generate and assess conjectures

Read the current question, conventions, observations, and relevant results. Seek a mathematical statement that explains something substantial in the evidence. Possible forms include a relation between invariants, an inequality, a recurrence, a decomposition, a classification, an obstruction, an asymptotic statement, or a uniform construction. Let the mathematical problem determine the form.

Consider whether the evidence suggests changing the original question or introducing a useful definition. A model's errors, generated examples, or partially interpreted computation can all provide motivation. A complete circuit explanation or proof strategy is not required before proposing a conjecture.

For each serious candidate, write a [conjecture record](../templates/CONJECTURE.md):

1. State the domain, definitions, hypotheses, quantifiers, and conclusion in standard mathematical language. Specify any restrictions on parameters. The mathematical statement should be understandable without access to a neural network or an experiment log.
2. Explain why the statement is natural and nontrivial. What relation would it clarify, what family would it organize, or what obstruction would it identify? Compare with immediate consequences of definitions and the closest available theorems or conjectures.
3. Identify the observations or arguments that motivated it, with links to examples and records. Say which ranges were exhaustive, sampled, or untested. Separate exact computations from numerical or statistical evidence.
4. Check obvious degeneracies and informative boundary cases where feasible. Record limitations honestly. Do not make exhaustive testing, proof-search failure, or a prescribed sample count a prerequisite for a conjecture motivated by substantial reasoning.
5. State the next useful question. It may concern a revealing family, a sharper formulation, an interpretation experiment, a subsidiary lemma, or a possible counterexample. A proof attempt is optional.

Assess precision, naturalness, mathematical content, evidence, and relation to existing work in a short paragraph. Explain the judgment; do not rely on an aggregate self-assigned score. Conjectures new to the project may be useful rediscoveries, but novelty in mathematics requires an appropriate comparison. Consult sources according to the project policy and state the boundary of that comparison.

Group equivalent statements and cosmetic variants. Do not count the same conjecture many times. If a candidate merely fits a finite list by construction, identify the missing reason to expect a meaningful general relation. If exceptions require a revised hypothesis, explain the structural reason for it and preserve the original statement and counterexample.

Report good conjectures as research outcomes while their proofs remain open. Keep status separate: proposed, empirically supported in a specified range, proved with a linked argument and review status, refuted with a checked counterexample, or recognized as known. These descriptions are not a mandatory linear progression. State what remains unresolved without inflating finite evidence into a theorem.
